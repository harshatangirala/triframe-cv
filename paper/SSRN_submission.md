**Title:** What Do Charts Add? Multi-Horizon Price Images and the Cross-Section of Equity Risk

**Author:** Krishna Harsha Tangirala, Independent Market Researcher and Trader

**Date written:** November 2, 2026

**Pages:** see the PDF

**Keywords:** volatility forecasting, computer vision, transfer learning, technical analysis, forecast evaluation, machine learning in finance

**JEL:** C45, C53, C58, G12, G17

**Suggested networks:** Financial Economics Network (Capital Markets: Market Efficiency; Econometric and Statistical Methods), Computer Science Research Network (Artificial Intelligence)

**Abstract:** I test whether images of price charts add to numeric features when forecasting stock risk. Using 236,520 weekly, daily and hourly chart sets for 500 US stocks from 2001 to 2022 and seven pretrained vision networks, I find no gain in pooled out-of-sample R² for 20-day log volatility (+0.003; 95% CI -0.018 to +0.026). This null hides a split. Images raise within-month cross-sectional R² by 0.020 (CI 0.012 to 0.027) but do not improve the forecast of the market-wide level, which accounts for 38% of squared error. Images do not improve drawdown ranking, and five-day direction remains unpredictable. Hourly realized volatility does not explain the image gain. The cross-sectional test was specified after the data were seen, and the universe contains only surviving firms.
